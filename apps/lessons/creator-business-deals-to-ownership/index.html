The creator business: from deals to ownership

Brand deals pay creators once. The post goes live, the money lands, and next month starts again at zero. Here is how the creator business makes money today, from the cash you need now to the assets that keep paying and can one day be sold.

Brand deals: the main income.

Brand deals are the main income of creators. A brand pays for a post, a story or a video, and the deal ends the day the content goes up. Stop posting, and the income stops.

Agencies: a cut for waiting.

Creators hand their deals to agencies. The agency finds brands, negotiates, and takes a percentage of every deal it signs. That is the theory, but in practice the agency waits for brands to come in, then takes its cut on deals the creator’s audience brought.

I tested them. I wrote to 30 agencies with a business opportunity for their creators. 3 replied, 1 booked a call, and that one never showed up, which shows how they treat any deal they have to chase.

Their job is to find opportunities. They let them pass.

High-ticket coaching: the best start.

High-ticket coaching is the best first business for a creator. The audience already trusts you. One launch ran 12 days and brought $60,000, which lifted that creator’s month to $83,000. The limit is time: every client takes hours from your week, so the income stops growing the day your calendar is full.

Programs and commissions.

Next come programs and commissions. A creator promotes a program built by someone else, and keeps a commission on each sale. It scales better than coaching, because one video sells to the whole audience at once, but the product, the customers and the brand belong to someone else.

Ownership: what scales.

Take the cash first. Brand deals and coaching pay the bills while you build. Scaling comes with ownership: a blog, a mobile app, a product that earns without your face on it and that a buyer would pay for.

An app can be sold. A personal brand needs you every day, while an app runs with your face out of it and keeps its value when you step away. And an app with a list and a blog is 3 assets.

Why I build apps with creators.

That is my bet. I build mobile apps with creators, because an app is the best leverage on the audience they spent years building. Making content is already a full-time job. So I run the app, they keep creating, and we partner for the long term.

Deals pay once. Ownership keeps paying.
