AN INTERACTIVE PLAYBOOK

THE REVENUE
MACHINE

DEFINITION
scale
verb. to make the same machine produce more, without turning it into a different machine.

Not the same as growth. Growth is one good month. Scale is a loop where the money that comes out reliably buys more of whatever put it there, and where you know which part of the loop you are pulling on. Almost everybody builds features, calls that product, and then wonders why the revenue never compounds.

This page is the operating manual for the machine behind a mobile subscription app. Not a list of tactics: the order they go in, and the reason each one only works once the one before it does. Volume gets people in front of the shelf. Conversion decides how many of them pay. Price sets what each one is worth. Survival decides how long that lasts. Revenue is the product of the four, which is why the biggest win is never in the term you are already best at.

Every number here comes out of the tasu conversion brain: RevenueCat's State of Subscription Apps 2026 (115,000 apps, $16B), Adapty's 2026 dataset (20,000+ apps), a study of 2,995 paywalls, and the operators who ship this for a living. Where a claim is one practitioner's judgement rather than a dataset, it says so, because knowing which is which is half the skill.

Ten steps, counting from zero, then one machine of your own. Five of them you can play with rather than just read.

START

Installs are
the easy part.

Steven Cravotta says it in six words and it is the only sentence on this page you have to accept before the rest works. Your app is not a product. Your app is a funnel. Getting installs is easy. Getting those installs to pay, and maximising what each one is worth, is the entire job.

The reason to take that seriously is the shape of the market. Across RevenueCat's 2026 dataset of 115,000 apps, of every new app launched, in its first two years 17.3% ever clear $1K a month, 4.6% clear $10K, and 1.7% clear $25K. One year after launch the median app earns about $72 a month. The top ten percent earn more than $2,574. Fewer than one in twenty new apps ever reaches what most founders assume is the starting line.

That distribution is not explained by idea quality. Everybody in it had an idea. What separates the tail is whether there is a machine underneath, and whether the person running it knows which lever is currently the binding one.

The machine has four terms and they multiply:

  • ▸VOLUME. How many people arrive. Your own content, other people's audiences, the store shelf, and paid on top once it is safe to turn on.
  • ▸CONVERSION. What fraction of them pay. Onboarding, then the paywall. Decided almost entirely on the day they install.
  • ▸PRICE. What one payer is worth per period. The term you are most likely underselling.
  • ▸SURVIVAL. How many periods they stay. The quiet term that sets the ceiling on everything above it.

Because they multiply, doubling your worst term beats doubling your best one, every single time. Most of the work of scaling revenue is not effort. It is correctly identifying which of the four is smallest this month, and refusing to touch the other three until it is not.

STEP 01 · VOLUME

Volume is
the whole game.

The first distribution move is to make the content yourself, and it stays the right move even if you are funded. Mau Baron's argument is not that it is cheap. It is that making it yourself develops the viral sense you will need later to direct influencers and UGC creators, so it is the foundational skill rather than the fallback.

The volume target is higher than almost anyone is running. Around 21 posts a day — seven originals, each cross-posted to TikTok, Instagram and YouTube Shorts. For the raw material: study your competition and copy it. Same hooks, same scripts, sometimes the same call to action. Originality at this layer is a tax you cannot afford yet.

THE FIVE FORMATS, TRADED AGAINST EACH OTHER

Each format sits somewhere on a line between how well it converts and how easily it can be produced in bulk. You want both ends running.

  • ▸UGC REACTION + DEMO. The one to start with, described as a format that never fails — it took Prayer Lock to $2.5K a month on its own. Buy reaction clips, stitch your demo onto them, keep the demo under eight seconds. The hook is the clip plus the overlay text, not the product.
  • ▸SLIDESHOWS. There are apps making $20K+ a month from slideshows alone, and the same assets double as paid ad creative. The requirement is one cohesive brand identity — not a Pinterest feed of random aesthetic images — plus a direct download CTA on every post.
  • ▸THE JACK FRIKS METHOD. A flattering, relatable identity label in the headline, then four image cards of traits or behaviours that qualify someone for it. Converts less. Bulk-produces beautifully.
  • ▸VIDEO HOOK + CTA. The most automatable of the five. Scrape viral hooks, take the first three seconds, cut entertainingly to your CTA. A script stitched 700 clips in thirty minutes and bulk-scheduled them.
  • ▸MEMES. Mostly brand awareness, strongest on Instagram when paired with a ManyChat automation and Stories that route a follower to the download.

THE PERMUTATION ENGINE

Ernesto's production trick is to split human UGC into two swappable halves. Record fifty reaction clips in one session. Record three body or demo clips. Then assemble permutations by independently swapping reaction, body and text hook. One afternoon of shooting yields weeks of posting.

The claim attached to it is that near-identical posts built this way do not get shadowbanned, because platforms keep pushing them as long as engagement signals are present. Hold that one loosely: it is a practitioner assertion with no dataset, and it sits directly against TikTok's own duplicate-content suppression. It is a boundary being ridden, not a free lunch.

The slideshow equivalent is a recipe, not an idea. Collect five to ten overperforming slideshows in your niche until the pattern is obvious — one example is a copy, ten is a pattern — then decode slide text, image description, whether the photos read as AI or real, caption, hashtags, and reproduce it. Mix AI images with real photographs, because a wholly AI set reads as fake. The volume benchmark is blunt: a few posts a day is a real account, one a day is a hobby.

THE 72 HOURS THAT DECIDE A NEW ACCOUNT

Every new TikTok account is trust-scored in its first three days, and failing it is reported to trigger a permanent shadowban. The sequence: days one and two are engagement only — scroll thirty minutes or more, like fifteen to twenty videos, leave five to seven comments, follow eight to ten accounts, post nothing, add no bio link. Day three is one non-promotional slideshow with trending audio. Over 1,000 views is a pass. Under 200 means burn it and start again.

The reason to run many accounts rather than one perfect one is that a big account going viral is audience-dependent and teaches you nothing. A micro-account with a thousand followers going viral is format-dependent — there is no audience advantage to explain it away, so the format did all the work. That is the only kind of virality you can copy, and you only find it by running enough attempts to see it twice. Once, and it is luck.

Finally, the compounding argument, which is an assertion rather than a measurement but worth holding anyway. Owned channels — the accounts, the email list, the store listing, the community — keep paying after you stop working on them. Paid acquisition stops the instant the card does. Build the compounding side first and use paid to accelerate a signal that already exists.

STEP 02 · PARTNERS

Renting somebody
else's audience.

Jake, from Cal AI, lays the three outside channels on one spectrum, and the useful part is that each trades a different thing away.

  • ▸UGC. Low cost — roughly $1K for thirty videos. High risk, no guaranteed reach. Asymmetric upside: one video landing can make the whole month profitable.
  • ▸INFLUENCERS. The middle. Guaranteed reach and the upside is preserved, but $1K+ a post, slow to scale, and the attribution is murky.
  • ▸PAID ADS. High cost, low risk, fully controllable, and the upside is capped at roughly $1.20 to $1.50 back per dollar.

Operators pick the wrong one for the wrong reason: UGC because it is cheap, paid because it is predictable, influencers because they are fashionable. The right reason is whether the niche structurally supports it. These are practitioner-reported figures with no dataset size attached, so treat them as shape rather than precision.

FOUR CONDITIONS BEFORE A DOLLAR MOVES

Score the niche before you spend on creators. Supply greater than demand — more creators than brands chasing them, which is what makes CPMs cheap. Seamless integration — does your product drop into the formats that already exist here, which is a product question and not a marketing one. Inherent virality — does sponsored content in this niche actually outperform organic, as Cal AI's did. Parasocial trust — do the fans feel like friends.

Three or four conditions met: run influencers. Two: workable with more runway. Zero or one: use UGC for the asymmetry or paid ads for the predictability, and leave creators alone. The fourth condition is the one people skip because it never shows up in the analytics. Both Jake and Mau Baron say you read it in the comments: are the replies just fire emojis, or do people actually care about this person?

THE OUTREACH IS A VOLUME GAME, THE CLOSE IS NOT

Once a niche passes, the playbook is unglamorous. Budget at least $10K, because most creators will not convert. Send a hundred DMs a day, keep them short, and open with something like "Paid Promo Opportunity" to cut through the noise. Filter hard: in your niche, real engagement, parasocial trust visible in the comments.

Then the rule that costs people money: never disclose the deal terms in a DM. It hands the creator time to reconsider and negotiate you down. Get them on a call, or at worst text the offer to their phone. Target as close to a $1 CPM as you can. Ernesto's concrete structure is a trial deal at $1 CPM for 300K views with 40% paid upfront, moving to $1.2 CPM once the creator has proven out. One team, one playbook, no dataset — but a specific one, which is more useful than a vague one.

OR MAKE THEM AN OWNER

The alternative to per-video fees is 20% to 50% of app revenue, which converts a vendor into a distribution partner. Lower upfront cost, higher long-term cost, aligned incentives instead of rented attention. George Lamb at Wrestle AI validated the 50/50 end of that range. Two cautions travel with it: due diligence on the partner matters enormously because you cannot easily undo it, and even an excellent partner's audience eventually saturates, so you still have to diversify.

THE UGC ARMY, AND THE PART NOBODY POSTS ABOUT

Hiring micro-creators to spin up accounts making content only for your app is, in Mau Baron's words, often called THE way to scale mobile apps right now — the highest-leverage channel and the hardest to crack. The recruiting filter is different from influencer outreach: source on TikTok, and ignore follower count entirely. Screen for two or three viral videos, which proves repeatability rather than a fluke, plus camera charisma and good communication. It is about taste and viral sense more than anything else.

The part X undersells is that it is a full-time job. You direct creators to the right content, check in constantly, track performance, hire continuously, fire fast, and pay everybody. His own advice is that a new builder should not attempt this solo without prior distribution experience or a cofounder. Get it working and it is the highest-leverage skill you can own. Underestimate the operations and it eats every hour you have.

STEP 03 · PAID

Ads are the
last channel.

Mau Baron took Prayer Lock from $2.5K a month to $25K a month at 50% profit margins, mostly on paid ads — and only turned them on after the organic content and the onboarding were already working. Most operators start paid either far too early or far too late, and the too-early failure is much more expensive.

Two gates have to clear first, and they are specific.

  • ▸GATE ONE · CREATIVE. A content system already producing organic winners. You need about five high-quality pieces a week to start testing — where high quality means it worked organically, not that it was expensively produced.
  • ▸GATE TWO · FUNNEL. At least a 10% download-to-trial rate. Below that the paid arithmetic does not close, and you are paying to fill a leaky funnel.

This is the one place where the two camps agree completely. The distribution-first people and the paywall-first people argue about which comes first; nobody argues that you should scale spend into a funnel you have not measured.

ORGANIC ADS, NOT ADS

What you pour into the ad account is not a new asset. It is the organic winner, given a clear direct call to action. That is the whole idea behind an "Organic Ad": a post with a genuinely organic hook that feels like a real organic video, but which asks for the download outright rather than converting vaguely through the comment section.

TikTok has a native version of this. Spark Ads — boosting the organic post itself — beat dark posts by 30% to 142% on completion and 37% to 69% on conversion rate.

THE AD ACCOUNT IS A RESEARCH LAB

Run spend through three phases in order. Discovery: find a creative that makes people care. This is not a ROAS test, and measuring ROAS here kills campaigns before they have done their job. Validation: confirm the winner drives real funnel events — paywall views, trial starts. Scale: move it into a trial or purchase campaign and scale slowly. ROAS is a phase-three metric. Every creative, including every failure, is diagnostic signal about what people want and where the funnel breaks.

The single most important setting is the optimization event, because the delivery algorithm returns whatever type of user you asked for. Ask for installs and you get window shoppers. Ask for trial starts and subscriptions and you get people who do those things. Most subscription apps spending $300 to $2,000 a day should optimize for Trial Start rather than Purchase, because Purchase is too rare to feed the algorithm.

Which sets the budget floor. An ad set needs roughly 50 optimization events in a rolling seven days to exit the learning phase; below that, CPAs run 20% to 50% above the post-learning average. So the minimum daily budget is (target CPA × 50) ÷ 7, and you optimize for the deepest event your volume can still support.

TIKTOK IS CHEAPER AND HUNGRIER

TikTok CPMs run $2.60 to $6.60, with a practitioner average of $4.52, against Meta's $9 to $15. But creative fatigue hits five times faster — two to seven days versus two to four weeks. On TikTok the constraint is how much creative you can produce, not how much you can spend, which is exactly why step 01 comes before step 03. UGC-style creative outperforms polished creative by about 55% on ROI. The minimum honest channel test is $5,000 over fourteen days; less than that and you have not tested TikTok, you have sampled it.

And the ceiling on all of it: LTV caps CAC. Whoever earns more per user can always outbid you in the auction, and the only way to outspend them is to out-earn them first. That is why the rest of this page exists.

STEP 04 · THE STORE

The shelf
nobody staffs.

App Store search drives roughly 65% of iOS discovery and roughly 58% on Google Play. Most teams give store-page work under 8% of growth headcount. That gap between where the installs come from and where the labour goes is the largest underexploited lever in mobile, and it is free.

THE ICON IS THE TAP GATE

Nothing else on the listing gets a chance if the icon does not earn the tap. Bold colour, one clear subject, readable at 60 by 60 points. The test is not whether you like it — it is whether, against the top twenty results for your target keyword, yours is the only one conveying the right idea. Apple's Product Page Optimization makes icon A/B testing free, so first drafts are not worth defending.

One cheap trick from the teardowns: a live year badge. Weight Loss for Women: Workout ships an icon with "2026" on it, refreshed annually, which answers the browser's unspoken question — is this thing still maintained, or abandoned? At 10M+ downloads, 446K ratings and 4.8 stars, they are not guessing.

METADATA HAS A STRICT HIERARCHY

Apple weighs the fields in order: Title (30 characters, highest weight), Subtitle (30), Keyword field (100, invisible, indexed), Description (not indexed for search at all). In-app purchase names are 30 characters each and are also indexed, which almost everybody misses. And never repeat a keyword across fields: Apple counts it once, and those wasted characters could have indexed a different term.

Two free capacity tricks. Apple indexes keywords across locales for a single storefront, so extra English terms dropped into the es-MX keyword field get the US storefront indexed for them too — roughly doubling capacity for nothing. And In-App Events create time-limited cards that appear in search results alongside your normal listing: a second visible item in the same result set, free to create, and most indie developers never use it.

THE LONG TAIL IS WHERE A SMALL APP CAN WIN

Semantic, NLP-driven ranking means a new app is never outranking Calm for "meditation". The same app can own "sleep stories for adults with anxiety" at three to five times higher conversion, because the intent behind an ultra-specific query is ultra-specific. Target thirty to fifty long-tail keywords rather than five to ten head terms. As PaulSolt puts it: we are not making an app you would pay for, we are making an app that solves a problem the user is searching for.

CUSTOM PRODUCT PAGES CHANGED THE GAME IN JULY 2025

Custom Product Pages used to be reachable only by direct URL or through Apple Search Ads. Since July 2025 they appear in organic search results for their assigned keywords — the biggest ASO mechanism change in years. Up to 70 per app, each with its own screenshots, headline and preview video. A fitness app can run separate store pages for "running", "strength training" and "yoga". Conversion lifts 5.9% when the creative tightly matches the keyword's intent.

That is also the discipline for Apple Search Ads: bid on the terms you have built a matching Custom Product Page for, and let paid and organic land on the same tailored page. Paid search on a generic store page is paying full price for the worst version of your listing. Note the honest limit — the tasu brain's evidence here is on the Custom Product Page mechanism and the ASO fields, not on ASA cost-per-install benchmarks, so build the page first and let your own account produce the cost numbers.

STEP 05 · DATA

Measure it,
or guess.

Onboarding gets measured screen by screen — entry rate, drop-off, next-screen rate — and then the rest of the app gets a DAU chart and nothing else. The correction is one sentence: every screen is a funnel step, with an entry event and an exit event. The same leak-finding that works between install and paywall works between any two screens in the product. The funnel does not end at the paywall, so the instrumentation should not either.

THE ONE NUMBER UPSTREAM OF EVERYTHING

Adapty's 2026 dataset of 20,000+ apps puts the single best predictor of revenue at the App Install → Paywall View rate. Revenue moves almost linearly with it: double that rate, and revenue roughly doubles. It is the highest-leverage number to track upstream of the paywall itself, and it is a number most dashboards do not show by default.

THE HEAT MAP THAT ACTUALLY SAYS SOMETHING

Render screen and feature usage intensity as a heat map, then segment it by user type: free, trial, paid, churned. The single-segment view is decoration. The value is in the difference — the screens paid users heat up and churned users never touched are your conversion and retention levers, and they tell you where to point onboarding, lifecycle pushes and the next build cycle.

BE HONEST ABOUT ATTRIBUTION

True view-through attribution for organic TikTok does not exist. MMP infrastructure was built for paid. Everything available is a workaround, in descending order of reliability: a UTM-tagged bio link, which is low friction but only captures bio clicks; deferred deep links through Branch, AppsFlyer or Airbridge, reliable on Android but only 30% to 40% capture on iOS because of ATT opt-out; and spike correlation against App Store Connect's daily install delta. The TikTok Pixel does not track app installs at all — it only fires on a website.

The practical conclusion: instrument the inside of your app to the decimal, because that part you fully control, and treat channel attribution as directional. A channel read that is 40% accurate is still enough to tell a winner from a dud if the inside of the funnel is exact.

THE RULE THAT DISPATCHES THE DAY

One comparison decides what kind of work today is: downloads today against the trailing seven-day average. Below it, the constraint is attention — go market. At or above it, the constraint is conversion — go optimize. The rule exists to kill the two default failure modes of a solo founder: optimizing a funnel nobody enters, and marketing into a funnel that leaks. It is an operating heuristic, not a measured law, and its whole value is that it makes the decision before your mood does.

Two guardrails on top. Below about 300 installs a day, no single test on your own app is statistically reliable — read direction, not decimals. And recheck your winning cohorts at three and six months, because refunds and chargebacks are silent killers that hide the true cost of a paywall experiment that looked like a win.

STEP 06 · ONBOARDING

Onboarding is
seventy percent.

"The onboarding is 70% of the app." That is a practitioner heuristic from @Jahjiren with no dataset behind it, and it is worth taking seriously anyway, because the measured data explains why it would be true.

RevenueCat's 2026 numbers are brutally front-loaded. 78% to 90% of trial starts happen on Day 0. About half of all paid conversions happen on Day 0 — 71.9% in Productivity. If a user does not start a trial or pay on the day they installed, they usually never will. So a bad onboarding does not convert badly once. It loses the user permanently, because nobody comes back to give an app a second first impression.

SIX JOBS, NOT SIX SCREENS

Across the teardowns in the tasu library, every high-converting flow decomposes into the same six jobs, in wildly different orders. Use it as a coverage checklist, never as a sequence.

  • ▸ATTRIBUTION. How did you hear about us.
  • ▸PERSONALIZATION. The questions that visibly shape the plan.
  • ▸PERMISSION. The notification ask, embedded in context.
  • ▸SOCIAL PROOF. Rating walls, expert endorsement, user counts.
  • ▸COMMITMENT. Micro-yeses, pledges, holds, a home-screen widget.
  • ▸PAYWALL. The ask itself.

A screen doing none of the six is a cut candidate. A flow skipping one of the six is leaving a known mechanism on the table. Note what is not on the list: screen count. Bend runs 22 screens at $1.1M a month. Finch runs 28 at $30M ARR. Brilliant runs 24. Duolingo runs 38. Airlearn runs 6 in the same category as Duolingo. Length is not the variable — what the screens are doing is.

THE SCREENS WORTH STEALING

  • ▸BEND HIDES THE PERMISSION IN A HABIT QUESTION. "When do you usually exercise?" — morning, lunchtime, evening — configures the reminder time, so the iOS prompt confirms a choice the user just made rather than asking cold. The teardown calls it the best single conversion screen in the series.
  • ▸BEND AND AIRLEARN BOTH INSTALL A WIDGET BEFORE THE PAYWALL. A physical home-screen commitment and a free retention touchpoint with zero notification fatigue, captured at peak first-session motivation.
  • ▸AIRLEARN REFRAMES PERMISSION AS IDENTITY. "Are you committed to learning Spanish?" fires before the system prompt does, so the grant is a declaration rather than an interruption.
  • ▸FINCH BUILDS THE BOND IN SIXTY SECONDS. Pick an egg, hatch it, name it, give it a personality — before any feature is shown. The paywall then does not ask you to buy a subscription. It asks you to keep everything you just built.
  • ▸BRILLIANT MAKES THE METHOD THE ONBOARDING. Screen one is an actual problem to solve, not a value-proposition slide. And the authority claim — inspired by the teaching methods of MIT, Harvard, Google and Microsoft — is dropped mid-flow, after the experience, where it reads as confirmation rather than a pitch.
  • ▸BEND ASKS ATTRIBUTION LAST. At the end of personalization rather than on screen two, betting that a user twenty screens deep recalls their channel more accurately, and that by then it reads as curiosity rather than a survey.

And one mistake to deliberately not copy: Bend fires the App Store review request after the forced questionnaire but before the paywall and before any product experience at all. It generates one-star reviews from users who never used the app — one of whom wrote that they were asked to rate it with no idea whether the app is any good.

STEP 07 · PAYWALL

The decision
point.

Steven Cravotta's framing: the paywall is the most important screen in your app because it is the decision point. Everything before it is preparation and everything after it is delivery. This is the screen where the machine either takes money or does not.

HARD BEATS SOFT, AND THE CAVEAT MATTERS

RevenueCat 2026, 115,000 apps: Day-35 download-to-paid is 10.7% for hard paywalls against 2.1% for freemium — roughly five times. Revenue per install runs eight to nine times higher (D14 $2.32 versus $0.27; D60 $3.09 versus $0.38). Adapty's 2026 dataset points the same way on LTV: median $24 hard versus $20 soft, widening to $89.9 against $70 at the ninetieth percentile.

Now the caveat from the same dataset, because it is the useful half. Year-one retention is nearly identical between the two models, and hard-paywall variance is enormous — a P90 of 38.7% and a floor of 4.2%. The label is not what makes the money. Execution is, and execution here means one specific thing: the investment you put in front of the wall.

Brilliant is the exemplar. A hard paywall — 7-day trial or exit, no free mobile tier — at $149.88 a year, arriving at the end of 24 screens in which the user solved a real problem, chose a goal, set a daily time commitment, and read two dedicated screens explaining the trial. At that point trial-or-exit reads as a conclusion. The identical wall shown cold is just a wall.

THREE SCREENS THAT REMOVE THE FEAR

Bill shock is the primary reason users bounce at a paywall, and it is defused before any price is shown. Screen one: try for free, no payment now. Screen two: we will remind you before your trial ends. Screen three: the paywall, restating both promises. Cali, at $2–3M a month, runs it; per a study of 200+ onboarding flows, so do all the top apps.

How much trust-building to do scales with the price. Split the two jobs across two screens — one for the timeline, when money actually moves, and one for the contents, what actually unlocks — so that the pricing screen only has to answer "is this worth it?" rather than "will I get charged?" and "what do I even get?" At $149.88 a year, that separation is worth two whole screens.

THERE IS NO BEST PAYWALL, ONLY BETTER EXPERIMENTS

Across 2,995 paywalls studied by Mobbin, the throughline is that no universal winner exists. And the tests that move the needle are design-level variants, not element tweaks: run a video paywall against a bullet-list paywall against a trial-timeline paywall against a long-form one, until something blows everything else out of the water. In one documented case an ugly, text-heavy squeeze page outperformed the polished designs — which is the whole argument for letting experiments, not taste, decide.

Then the number that should reorganise your calendar. Apps running 50+ paywall experiments a year earn 18.7× more revenue than apps running one, at the same category and the same price point. Top performers average around fifteen a year. Most founders run zero or one and treat the paywall as a static checkout screen. It is not about time, it is about volume — you can A/B test a paywall in an hour, or you can take a year over it.

Velocity is bought, and it has a price. Duolingo cut its free trial from 14 days to 7 specifically to double experimentation velocity, accepting a hit to per-trial conversion to get a faster feedback loop. The trade is visible in the same dataset: trial-to-paid runs 25.5% at four days or under, 37.4% at five to nine days, and 42.5% at seventeen to thirty-two days. Shorter trial, weaker conversion, more experiments per quarter. Which side of that you want depends entirely on whether your bottleneck is conversion or learning rate.

THE EXIT IS ALSO REVENUE

About 70% of users who see a hard paywall do not convert, and most never come back on their own. Three documented ways to monetise that tail. Bend fires a one-time gift on skip — $57 off the annual plan, $89 down to about $32, stated as gone forever, and it genuinely never reappears. PuffCount recovered $12,000 by pushing a 30% discount to non-converters, at essentially zero marginal cost since there was no incremental spend on users who were already gone. And an ad served only after a full skip monetises an otherwise-lost install while previewing what the free experience feels like.

The line that separates that last one from a trust violation is placement, not the ad itself. After a full opt-out it is a fair transaction. Inside the core ritual of a paying user, it is a withdrawal from an account you cannot easily refill.

STEP 08 · PRICE

Charge
more.

The counter-intuitive finding, and it holds on both axes at once: higher-priced apps convert better AND earn more per user. Racing to the cheapest price in your category loses twice.

RevenueCat 2026, on conversion: Day-35 download-to-paid runs 2.8% for high-priced apps, 2.0% mid, 1.4% low — and the top quartile of high-priced apps hits 6.1%. On value: monthly revenue-LTV per payer is $35.89 high, $15.78 mid, $6.67 low, a 5.4× gap that widens to roughly 6× by year one ($62.19 against $10.69).

The mechanism is self-selection. A user who has walked all the way to a high-priced paywall has already decided the category is worth paying for. A cheap price does not attract more buyers, it attracts more browsers.

At the extreme, an anecdote: Jurney moved from $0.99 a week with a 3-day trial to $14.99 a week with no trial, and tripled revenue. "I thought higher prices would scare people away. I was wrong. I was charging like I didn't believe in my own app." Read it as an anecdote — that change bundled a price rise with trial removal, so it is not a clean price-only test.

STRUCTURE MOVES MORE THAN PRICE

Adapty's 2026 dataset of 20,000+ apps: weekly plans convert two to seven times better than annual and ten times better than monthly. Install-to-trial runs 5.4% to 9.9% on weekly against 0.3% to 0.6% on monthly. Monthly is a graveyard at any price point — never lead with it. Every winning setup in that data involves weekly, and the single most common winner is a weekly plan at $5.99 with a 3-day trial. Two more counter-intuitive findings ride along: weekly conversion rises as price goes up, because weekly reads as low-commitment regardless of the absolute number, and higher-priced weekly tiers retain better at first renewal (54% against 48%).

And now the honest tension, because three good apps read that same benchmark and made three different calls.

  • ▸YARNPAL cites the weekly finding explicitly and overrides it, choosing annual because it bets its Lifestyle-category learners want a sustained journey rather than a weekly renewal decision.
  • ▸BRILLIANT shows annual only, at $149.88. Displaying the $24.99 monthly beside it would anchor the annual as large; showing the annual as $12.49 a month removes the anchor entirely.
  • ▸BEND shows both — $13.99 monthly and $39.99 annual, surfaced as $3.33 a month, a 76% discount on the per-month rate, with annual marked most popular.

The condition that decides it is whether your category is a job or a journey. A job — track this, convert that, fix this once — tolerates and often prefers the weekly loop. A journey the user is supposed to still be on in nine months is undermined by asking them to re-decide every seven days.

THE CEILING YOU CANNOT OUT-EXECUTE

Category and geography set bounds that no paywall test escapes. Revenue per install at D14 and D60: Health & Fitness $0.48 and $0.66, against Gaming's $0.08 and $0.14. North America $0.38 and $0.55, against India and Southeast Asia's $0.08 and $0.11 — a 5× geographic gap on the same product. Benchmark yourself against your own category, geography, price tier and access model, because a single global median will always flatter or insult you for the wrong reason.

AND THE FLOOR THAT MATTERS MORE

LTV is price multiplied by the area under the survival curve. Near the pricing optimum, the derivative with respect to engagement dwarfs the derivative with respect to price. The number-one stated cancellation reason is not enough usage; price barely makes the list. Price is the ceiling. Activation is the floor. Raise the price — and then go earn it, which is the next step.

STEP 09 · RETENTION

The leak under
the machine.

The sale is not where subscription money is made. The renewal is. Everything above this section decides how many payers walk in the door; this section decides how many months each of them is worth, which is the term the other three get multiplied by.

D7 IS THE EARLIEST HONEST SIGNAL

Week-one retention — the share of users still opening the app seven days after signup — is the first leading indicator that a habit is forming, and it arrives long before any downstream conversion metric can tell you anything. One tracked example: Amy, a calorie tracker at five months old and $2.8K MRR, running 13% D7. That is one small app's self-reported number, so treat it as directional rather than a category benchmark. The drivers, in priority order, are unglamorous: accuracy of the core output, a frictionless core loop, and an absence of bugs.

AI APPS SELL AND DO NOT STICK

RevenueCat 2026: AI-powered apps generate about 41% more revenue per payer and their subscribers churn roughly 30% faster. Hype drives the first sale, not the renewal. If you are building an AI app, retention rather than conversion is the metric that decides whether you own a category or ride a wave — which means solving it early, with a real habit and a use case that gets better as the model does, rather than a novelty people try once.

A THIRD OF ANDROID CHURN IS NOT A DECISION

Roughly 31% of Google Play subscription cancellations are involuntary billing failures — an expired card, a silently failed renewal charge — against about 14% on the App Store. That leak is invisible on a paywall-obsessed dashboard and it requires no product change to fix: grace periods, retry logic, account hold, update-payment prompts. For an Android-heavy app it is the highest-leverage, lowest-effort retention work available. In RevenueCat's words, fixing billing is the new growth hack.

EMAIL IS A RECOVERY CHANNEL, NOT AN ACQUISITION ONE

Mobile churn is silent: people stop opening without ever cancelling. Email is the primary out-of-app way to reach them before the next billing cycle. The window is specific — 35% of annual cancellations happen in Month 1. And once an annual subscriber has actually cancelled, reactivation drops to about 5%. So email's job here is pre-cancellation recovery, not post-cancellation win-back. Most app founders never build it at all.

THE UNSUBSCRIBE TAP IS AN INTERVENTION

Two mechanics, both running the same sunk-cost logic that onboarding uses, in reverse. An exit survey feeding a reason-matched offer: too expensive gets a discount, not using it gets a pause, missing a feature gets that feature highlighted — asymmetric upside, since the user was leaving anyway. Or friction as retention, where each completed step of a cancel questionnaire makes abandoning it harder. ClassPass shows how far that can be pushed: fewer than five screens to subscribe, seventeen to cancel.

THE MECHANICS THAT DO NOT BURN THE USER

Finch runs its streak on the bird's energy bar rather than the user's discipline. Completed goals fill the bar; a full bar sends the bird on a six-hour adventure that comes back with a discovery; a missed day drains the bird's energy rather than resetting a personal counter. That single design choice removes the guilt that drives uninstalls in conventional streak apps — at $30M ARR, 50M+ users and $0 raised.

Pillo donates HEART points to charity at 100% medication adherence, which reframes compliance as contribution rather than discipline and is the most durable motivation frame in the whole teardown series. Pillo also demonstrates the opposite: ads placed inside the medication alarm, replacing the calming sound the user chose during onboarding with audio from an advertised game. The onboarding gave the user agency and the ad took it back. It is the most significant trust violation documented in the library, and it is the reason a 570K-download app with a 4.76 rating has unclear revenue.

WHAT SURVIVES EVERY DISAGREEMENT

Five things the
operators all say.

They contradict each other constantly on entry points and on formats. These five survive all of it.

01Sequence beats effort. Cravotta says paywall, then onboarding, then marketing. Mau Baron says distribution first, then paid. They are arguing about the entry point and agreeing completely on the rule underneath: never scale spend into a funnel you have not measured.
02Volume, not time. 50+ paywall tests a year against one is an 18.7× revenue gap. Twenty-one posts a day, not three a week. The clock is not what produces the signal — the count is.
03One number dispatches the day. Downloads today against the trailing seven-day average, and it decides whether today is a marketing day or an optimizing day before your mood does.
04Nothing works until Day 0 works. Between 78% and 90% of trial starts, and about half of all paid conversions, happen on the day of install. Everything downstream is playing for the remainder.
05Charge more than is comfortable, then go earn it. Price is the ceiling and activation is the floor, and the ceiling is worth nothing if the floor is missing.

The uncomfortable one is the second. Most founders' real bottleneck is not insight. It is that they run one experiment where the data says fifty.

THE FOUR TERMS, ALL MULTIPLYING AT ONCE

TERM 1 · VOLUME — HOW MANY ARRIVE
Your own content at twenty-one posts a day, other people's audiences bought at roughly $1 CPM, the store shelf that carries 65% of iOS discovery for free, and paid on top once both gates are open. Slowest to build, and the only one that compounds after you stop.
TERM 2 · CONVERSION — WHAT FRACTION PAY
Onboarding then paywall, settled almost entirely on Day 0, where 78–90% of trials start. Hard paywalls convert 5× better, and the variance inside that number is decided by how much investment sits in front of the wall. Fifty experiments a year, not one.
TERM 3 · PRICE — WHAT A PAYER IS WORTH PER PERIOD
Higher prices convert better and earn more, 2.8% against 1.4% at D35 and $35.89 against $6.67 per payer. Weekly beats monthly tenfold on conversion — unless your category is a journey rather than a job, in which case annual wins and you should say so out loud.
TERM 4 · SURVIVAL — HOW MANY PERIODS THEY STAY
D7 as the first honest signal. A third of Android churn is a failed card, not a decision. 35% of annual cancellations land in Month 1, and reactivation after the cancel is about 5%. This is the term everything else gets multiplied by, and the only one nobody posts about.

Volume fills it. Conversion narrows it. Price scales it. Survival multiplies it. If you remember one line off this page, remember that the biggest win is never in the term you are best at. Find your smallest number and go there.
