My copywriting rules

  1. Write at a 3rd grade reading level. Use short, common words.
  2. Use periods or colons. Allow one comma per sentence. Skip em-dashes.
  3. Write in waves. Each length has one job:
    • Short (3 to 8 words): hook the reader, or break after a heavy point.
    • Medium (9 to 16 words): carry the explanation.
    • Long (17 to 25 words): deliver the serious point, with energy.

    Pattern: short hook, medium build, long payoff, short break. Repeat.

    Never write more than 2 sentences of the same length in a row.

    Every sentence stays under 25 words.

  4. Open with a short sentence that states a number, a result or a tension.
  5. Replace adjectives with data. “Fast” becomes “loads in 1.2 seconds”.
  6. Cut weasel words: very, actually, really, maybe, quite, some, many, probably, kind of.
  7. Define every vague word. If a word can mean 2 things, replace it with a number, a name or an example.
  8. Run the “so what” test on each sentence. If the reader can ask “so what?”, rewrite it with a result or a number.
  9. Say your best idea 5 different ways across the piece: a fact, a number, an example, a comparison, a call to action.
  10. End on one full sentence. Keep the last line whole, with no split into two short lines.
  11. Say what we do. Mention what we don’t do only if asked.
  12. Make them feel the emotion, without naming it. Let the experience you describe and your talking style carry the feeling.
