1 style for your app, in every update

Set your palette, fonts and rules first. Then every new screen, update and web page your AI builds looks like the same app.

The 6 principles in the prompts adapt the brand style guide method of Emilie Rouchy, art director, shared on Instagram.

Start with your palette

Your style guide is the rulebook your AI follows. The palette comes first: it decides what people notice on every screen. Have these ready:

  • Your app’s job and its audience, in 1 sentence.
  • 1 color you already own, a logo or a brand, or 3 words for the feeling you want.
  • 2 or 3 apps you like the look of. The 12 on tasu.ai are a start.
  • Light, dark or both.

Create my palette

Use it when you start your style guide. Your AI asks 5 questions, then writes every color with its role.

Create the color palette of my app. Ask me what you need first, then write it. Ask me, 1 question at a time: 1. What does the app do, and for whom? 2. Which color do I already own: a logo, a brand, a favorite? 3. Which 3 words should people feel when they open it? 4. Which 2 or 3 apps do I like the look of? 5. Light, dark or both? Then give me: - 1 pop color for the next action, used once per screen. - 1 structure color for icons, links and chosen states. - 1 balance color, for 1 calm shape per screen. - Neutrals: a background, a quiet fill, 3 text grays and 1 gray for icons only. - 3 tints of each color, for cards, chips and done states. For each color: name, HEX, RGB, role and where it never goes. Then: - The pairs that work, with their contrast. Text needs 4.5:1, large text and icons 3:1. - The colors I must never use. - The tokens as code for my stack. Check: every text pair passes, and each screen holds 1 pop element.

Set this first, then build

Write the guide before the next feature. Each update then reuses the same palette, fonts and rules. The app and its website stay 1 product, release after release.

The test. Give the guide to Claude Code and ask for a new screen. If the screen is right on the first try, the guide works.

Make every element look like 1 app

8 rules for each button, chip or card you add. The examples are HealthClass’s own values.

  1. 1

    Build from the tokens only

    Every element takes its colors, fonts, spacing and corners from the style guide. A new hex or a new size starts a second style.

    Every HealthClass pill rounds at half its height: 54 and 27 pt, 40 and 20, 30 and 15, 22 and 11.

  2. 2

    1 job, 1 look

    Each job gets 1 element, and each element 1 style. 2 looks for 1 job read as 2 apps.

    The next action is always the rose button. A second action is always a plum text link.

  3. 3

    A short size ladder

    Pick 4 heights and reuse them. Keep every touch area at 44 pt or more.

    54 pt for the button, 40 for the small button, 30 for a chip, 22 for a pill.

  4. 4

    Every state, written down

    Give each element its colors for default, pressed, disabled, selected and loading, and check each pair for contrast.

    A button that is off turns rose tint with rose dark text, at 5.2:1.

  5. 5

    1 press for every element

    Use the same scale, speed and spring everywhere, so the whole app feels like 1 material.

    Every button sinks to 96% in 90 ms and springs back in 420 ms.

  6. 6

    1 icon family

    The same grid, stroke and line ends for every icon, in 2 or 3 sizes.

    A 24 grid, a 1.75 px line and round ends: plum by default, rose when active.

  7. 7

    Shapes only where the eye must go

    A filled shape says “look here”. Give it to 3 or 4 things, and set the rest on the background.

    4 things get a shape: the rose button, the day circle, a chosen answer and the insight tiles. Lists are rows on the paper.

  8. 8

    Reuse before you add

    A new element needs a job no other element does, and its spec in the style guide before any code.

The spec of a new element

  1. Its 1 job, and where it appears
  2. Size: height and sides from the size ladder, a touch area of 44 pt or more
  3. Corner, from the corner rule
  4. Colors for each state, from the palette roles
  5. Type: 1 of the 4 levels
  6. Icon: from the family, at 1 of its sizes
  7. Press: the same as every other element
  8. 2 don’ts

Create a new element

Use it when you need a button, card or chip your app does not have yet.

Create a new element for my app that fits DESIGN.md. Read DESIGN.md and the components I already have first. 1. Tell me the element’s 1 job. If an existing element already does it, reuse that one and stop. 2. Write its spec in DESIGN.md: - Its job and where it appears. - Size: height and sides from the size ladder, a touch area of 44 pt or more. - Corner, from the corner rule. - Colors for each state, from the palette roles: default, pressed, disabled, selected, loading. - Type: 1 of the 4 levels. - Icon: from the icon family, at 1 of its sizes. - Press: the same as every other element. - 2 don’ts. 3. Build it once, as a reusable component, with tokens only. 4. Show it next to 2 existing elements, on the same screen. Check: - Zero values outside the tokens. - Every state’s text passes 4.5:1. - It looks like it was always part of the app.

Let your AI write the rest

Start from scratch

Use it when you have no app yet, or you want a new look. Your AI interviews you first.

Help me create my app’s style guide from scratch. Interview me first, then write DESIGN.md. Ask me 8 questions, 1 at a time, and wait for each answer: 1. What does the app do, for whom, in 1 sentence? 2. Which 3 words should people feel when they open it? 3. Which color, logo or brand do I already have? 4. Which 2 or 3 apps do I like the look of, and why? 5. Light, dark or both? 6. Calm and spacious, or dense and fast? 7. Rounded and soft, or sharp and precise? 8. Which screen matters most: the first one, the main action or the paywall? Then propose 2 directions in 5 lines each: palette, fonts, corners, density and 1 signature element. Let me pick 1 or mix them. Write DESIGN.md with 6 principles: 1. A number in every rule: “2 fonts”, “3 colors per screen”, “1 main button”. 2. A role for every element: each color and font has 1 job, written next to it. 3. A closed set: 3 colors plus neutrals, 2 fonts, 1 corner scale. 4. A don’t for every rule: write what never happens. 5. A version for every context: light and dark, the icon in color and in 1 color. 6. One sample screen that shows every rule at once. Sections, in this order: - Colors: HEX, RGB, role, the pairs and their contrast. - Type: 2 fonts, 4 levels with size and weight. - Icon: full color, 1 color, on light, on dark. - Components: sizes, corners, spacing and states. - Composition: 5 rules for 1 sample screen. Add 3 don’ts per section. Check: someone who never saw the app can build a new screen from DESIGN.md alone.

Use it when your app exists. Your AI reads it, finds the style it is trying to have, and asks only what the code cannot tell.

Apply my style guide to every screen

Use it when DESIGN.md exists, and the screens do not follow it yet.

Apply DESIGN.md to every screen of my app. Keep features, data and copy. 1. Turn every color, font, size, corner and spacing of DESIGN.md into a named token, in 1 file. 2. Replace every hard-coded value in the code with its token. For a value with no token, use the closest one and list it. 3. Go screen by screen, in this order: onboarding, home, the main action, settings, the paywall. 4. On each screen, apply the 5 composition rules: 1 hero, 3 colors at most, 1 main button, equal margins, the title and the tabs in their fixed spots. 5. Add the dark version of every token that DESIGN.md defines. 6. Fix every don’t you find, and name the rule it broke. Report per screen: rule, before, after. Check: - Zero hard-coded colors and font sizes in views. - 1 main button per screen. - Text passes 4.5:1 on its background, in light and dark.

Check my screens against my style guide

Use it when before a release, to catch what drifted.

Audit my app against DESIGN.md. Change no code. For each screen: - Colors: list every color used. Flag values outside the tokens, and an accent that covers more than 10% of the screen. - Type: flag a 3rd font, a size outside the scale and a heading that breaks the hierarchy. - Components: flag a corner, padding or height outside the scales, and a missing state (pressed, disabled, empty, error). - Composition: count the heroes and the main buttons, and flag more than 1 of each. Flag uneven margins, and a title or tab bar out of place. - Contrast: flag text under 4.5:1 and large text under 3:1, in light and dark. - Don’ts: flag every don’t of DESIGN.md that the screen shows. Then: 1. Rank the screens by number of issues. 2. Give the 5 fixes that remove the most issues. 3. Propose rules for DESIGN.md where the screens agree but the guide says nothing. Report as a table: screen, rule, what breaks it, the fix.
