Paywall tips from 12 top apps

7 paywall tips from 12 top-grossing iPhone apps and the 113 In-App Purchases they list on the US App Store, 15 Nov 2026. Each tip gives the numbers, the apps behind them and a prompt that applies it to your paywall.

App Store data, US, 15 Nov 2026

7 of 12state a free trial

Offer a 7-day free trial

7 of 12 apps state a free trial, in the App Store description or on their web pricing page. 4 of the 6 that give a length say 7 days: Ladder, Opal, MyFitnessPal and Rise. Duolingo gives 14 days and Strava 30.

LadderRiseDuolingoStrava

Prompt

Give my paywall a 7-day free trial. Find where my app defines its plans: StoreKit, RevenueCat, Adapty, Superwall or my own code. Attach a 7-day free introductory offer to the yearly plan. On the paywall, state the terms in one line next to that plan: “7 days free, then [yearly price] a year”. Read that price from the store at runtime. Show the trial line only to users the store marks as eligible. List every file you changed and each App Store Connect step I do by hand. Check: an eligible user sees “7 days free” and the price after it above the main button.

50%median yearly saving against monthly

Price a year at 6 monthly payments

All 7 apps that list a yearly plan also list a monthly one. A year costs 4 monthly payments at MyFitnessPal, a 67% saving, and 8 at Hevy and Huckleberry, a 33% saving. The median is 6 payments, a 50% saving: Ladder lists $179.99 a year and $29.99 a month.

MyFitnessPalHevyHuckleberryLadder

Prompt

Set my yearly price at 6 monthly payments, a 50% saving. Find where my app defines its plans: StoreKit, RevenueCat, Adapty, Superwall or my own code. Multiply my monthly price by 6 and round to the nearest price that ends in .99. Propose that yearly price and wait for my yes before you change it in the store. Compute the saving the paywall shows from both store prices at runtime: 1 minus the yearly price over 12 monthly payments. Check: the yearly price equals 4 to 8 monthly payments, and the saving on screen matches the math.

$79.99median yearly price, 7 apps

Check your yearly price against $79.99

7 apps list a yearly plan, from $23.99 at Hevy to $179.99 at Ladder. The median is $79.99, listed by Strava and MyFitnessPal; the monthly median is $14.99. 5 of the 7 sit between $69.99 and $119.99.

HevyStravaMyFitnessPalLadder

Prompt

Compare my prices with 7 top-grossing iPhone apps. Their yearly plans: Hevy $23.99, Headspace $69.99, Strava and MyFitnessPal $79.99, Opal $99.99, Huckleberry $119.99, Ladder $179.99. Find my prices in StoreKit, RevenueCat, Adapty, Superwall or my own code. Report my yearly price against the $79.99 median and my monthly price against the $14.99 median. Name the app closest to my yearly price. If mine sits outside $69.99 to $119.99, where 5 of the 7 sit, list the 3 features that justify the gap. Report only: I set prices myself. Check: the report states my yearly price, the $79.99 median and the gap in dollars.

1.8xsecond tier over the first, yearly

Price a second tier at 1.8x the first

7 of 12 apps list one tier. Huckleberry Premium, Ladder PRO+ and Strava + Runna cost 1.74x, 1.83x and 1.88x the first tier, yearly. Strava Family lands in the same band: $139.99 a year, 1.75x the $79.99 plan.

HuckleberryLadderStrava

Prompt

Plan a second tier for my app at 1.8x the yearly price of the first. Find where my app defines its plans: StoreKit, RevenueCat, Adapty, Superwall or my own code. Keep every feature my current subscribers have in the first tier. Name 2 or 3 features worth the higher price, from my roadmap or the requests of my most active users. Multiply my yearly price by 1.8 and round to a price that ends in .99. Draft both tiers for the paywall: name, yearly price and features. Wait for my yes before you create a product. Check: the second tier’s yearly price sits between 1.74x and 1.88x the first.

33 of 113listed purchases name their length

Put the plan length in every name

Hevy names all 4 listed purchases by length, from Monthly to Lifetime, and Opal names 9 of 10, like “Yearly Plan” at $99.99. 6 of 12 apps list bare names. Finch lists “Finch Plus” 9 times, at 8 prices from $5.99 to $69.99.

HevyOpalFinch

Prompt

Rename my In-App Purchases so each name states its length. Find my products in StoreKit, RevenueCat, Adapty, Superwall, my own code or an App Store Connect export. Write each display name as tier plus length, like “Pro Yearly” or “Pro Monthly”, in 30 characters or fewer. When 2 products share a tier and a length, add what differs, like “Pro Yearly Intro”. Output a table: product ID, current name, new name, length, price. Update my StoreKit file and paywall strings to match, and list the App Store Connect edits I make by hand. Check: every new name in the table contains Weekly, Monthly, Yearly or Lifetime.

111 of 113listed prices end in .99

End every price in .99

11 of 12 apps end every listed price in .99, from $0.99 at Duolingo and Cal AI to $479.99 at Ladder. Flo lists 2 purchases at $7.49 and $11.49, and 8 at prices that end in .99.

DuolingoCal AILadderFlo

Prompt

Make every price in my app end in .99. Find each price in StoreKit, RevenueCat, Adapty, Superwall, my own code and the paywall strings. List each one with its product ID and length. For a price with another ending, propose the .99 price under it, like $4.99 for $5.00. Replace every hard-coded price on the paywall with the localized price the store returns. Wait for my yes before you change a store price. Check: every price in the list ends in .99, and the paywall shows store prices only.

2 of 12list one-time purchases

Start one-time add-ons at $0.99

2 of 12 apps list one-time purchases next to the subscription. Cal AI lists a $0.99 Streak Restore. Duolingo lists 4 gem packs from $0.99 to $19.99; the $4.99, $9.99 and $19.99 packs give 240, 300 and 325 gems per dollar.

Cal AIDuolingo

Prompt

Add one consumable In-App Purchase at $0.99 next to my subscription. Find where my app defines its products: StoreKit, RevenueCat, Adapty, Superwall or my own code. Pick one action users repeat, like restoring a lost streak or one extra scan. Draft the product ID, display name, $0.99 price and the screen that offers it. If I sell packs, give bigger packs more units per dollar, like Duolingo’s 240, 300 and 325 gems per $1. Keep the subscription as the first offer on the paywall. Check: the add-on costs $0.99 and the paywall opens on the subscription.
