Architecture

What I read, what I concluded, and what I actually own.

This is the part of the site that is not about shipping apps. It is a standing piece of research on how wealth gets built and held through a turning era — written out from first principles, applied country by country, and then taught as something you can play with rather than read.

It exists because the advice I was given did not survive contact with the question "why would that keep working?". So I went back to three people who answer that question differently — Ray Dalio, Charles Gave, Richard Détente — arranged what survives all three, and published the structure so it can be argued with.

The book

Research is cheap to publish and easy to write in a direction nobody can check. So here is what is behind it. These are the positions summarised in the ledger pinned to the front page — what each one is, why it is in the book, and what it is supposed to do that the others cannot.

Medical SaaS

Private equity ~€1,100per month

A minority stake in a software business selling into medical practices, taken in 2023. Unglamorous, contracted, and almost entirely indifferent to the macro cycle — which is the reason it is here. Practices do not stop billing because the Fed is trapped, so the distribution keeps arriving in weather that would stop a listed position cold.

The cost of that steadiness is that it cannot be sold in an afternoon and cannot be sized up on conviction. It is a slow asset doing a slow job.

Datacenter

Compute & energy ~€2,100per month

The compute-and-energy pocket, entered in 2026 rather than researched from a distance. The thesis is the one set out in principle 13: compute is becoming an asset class in the formal sense, and the real constraint is not chips but the power to run them — which pushes the value down the stack into energy.

It is also the most dangerous thing here and is sized as such. New asset classes are where the returns and the failures live, in the same place, for the same reason. What makes it worth holding next to the others is that it is driven by AI capex and electricity supply — a physical build-out that keeps running through monetary conditions that would stop a normal cycle.

Liquid portfolio

Liquid ~35%as pinned on the front page

The only pocket that changes when the macro changes. Listed instruments, daily liquidity, sized by conviction, with every position carrying a written reason to exist and a written condition that ends it. It reads the quadrant off the country map and acts on it.

It runs as a barbell rather than a balanced fund: a protective layer that holds the line while the regime is unclear — gold and a currency run by adults — and an offensive layer expressing whatever the current quadrant is actually rewarding. It is the fastest pocket and the one most exposed to being wrong quickly, which is precisely why it needs the other two rather than more of itself.

The shape it makes

The research argues for four separate pockets rather than one pot — a small number of holdings, each bold on its own terms, none of them moving for the same reason. Here is how much of that shape actually exists yet, and where it is still only research.

LiquidEngine

Expresses the current quadrant. The only pocket that changes when the macro changes, and the only one where a position is a public, checkable claim rather than a private arrangement.

Held · published positions

Compute & energyEngine

The asset class being born right now. Energy first, hardware second, software third — because the constraint is power, not silicon.

Held since 2026

PropertyCollateral

Real collateral in the hinge jurisdictions — the places where capital from both blocs can still meet. The thing that still exists when the financial layer is having a bad decade.

Research only · not held

BitcoinAmplification

A bet that the thing everyone converges on when they cannot coordinate is changing. Sized off the gold ratio, which lands in the low single digits — small enough that being wrong is survivable, large enough that being right is material.

Research only · not disclosed

Where the line sits

Only the liquid pocket carries published positions. The private holdings above are described rather than itemised, and the two pockets marked research are exactly that — a view on how the pocket should be built, not a disclosure of what I own. Where that line sits matters, so I would rather state it than let the layout imply otherwise.

None of this is advice, and I am not a financial adviser. It is a structure I find defensible, published so it can be argued with.
