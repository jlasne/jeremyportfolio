A beginner's map

Wealth Architecture

How to think about money when the world changes. Built from Ray Dalio, Charles Gave and Richard Détente.

15 steps12 min read1 schema
00

Start with one question

Will tomorrow look like yesterday?

Your answer picks your assets. In a stable world, the recipes of the last 40 years keep working. In a changing world, the same recipes lose. Richard Détente calls this the first choice, and it is yours alone. The 15 steps below help you answer it, then act on it.

01

The long picture

Ray Dalio
1

Printed money lifts real things.

In 1933, and again in 1971, the United States stopped exchanging dollars for gold. Both times the stock market rose. After 1971 it gained close to 25% within a year. The same move happened in 2008 and in 2020.

The rule: when a central bank prints to fix a crisis, hold stocks, gold and commodities. Paper money loses value.

Dalio
2

Empires run in cycles of about 250 years.

Dalio studied 10 empires over 500 years. Each rose, peaked and declined in a similar order. Eight things measure the power: education, invention, competitiveness, output, share of world trade, military strength, the financial center, and the reserve currency.

Education leads the rise. The reserve currency is the last to go.

Dalio
3

Three signs say we are late in the cycle.

Dalio watches for three things together: debt too large to repay, so the central bank prints; a wealth gap that turns into populism; and a rising power facing the leading power. All three are present today.

The last time all three lined up was 1930 to 1945. A change of order takes 10 to 20 years.

Dalio
4

Money dies. Plan for it.

Détente puts the working life of a currency near 70 years. The dollar system began in 1944. With 8% of new dollars a year, a portfolio in dollars needs more than 8% a year to hold its value. Every product sold as "prudent" pays under 8%.

Where are we now?

Charles Gave

Gave
6

Read two dials set by millions of trades.

Growth dial: the S&P 500 divided by the oil price, averaged over 7 years. Gave's idea is that the economy is transformed energy. When companies turn oil into profit, the ratio rises.

Inflation dial: gold divided by long government bonds, averaged over 7 years. Gold winning means people trust metal over the state. Bonds won from 1980 to 2020. Gold won in the 1970s and wins again since 2020.

Millions of trades set these two prices, so they stay honest. Seven years filters the noise: in Gave's 100-year test, shorter windows gave false signals.

Gave
7

Choose by exclusion.

Four assets: stocks, bonds, gold, cash. In each square, one or two of them lose. Gave removes those and holds the other two or three. His reason: spotting the loser is easier than picking the winner.

It is how a bettor beats the track: cross out the horses with zero chance, then bet on the rest. The job of managing money is to avoid the 45% to 80% losses that take 10 years to repair.

Gave
8

When inflation rises, shorten your horizon.

Without inflation, buyers pay 20 years of profit for a stock. With inflation they pay 15, then 10, then 5. A company can keep the same profit and lose three quarters of its price.

So hold what pays you soon: companies with 4% to 7% dividends and sound management. Nestlé pays near 5%, Exxon near 4%. Swiss bonds pay 1%.

Every big bear market since 1945 began with a jump in the energy price. Gave holds 25% in energy stocks. They gain when oil gets expensive, and they pay the dividend.

Gave
03

Risk is the wrong word

Détente · Gave
9

Separate risk from danger.

Finance calls price movement "risk". The S&P 500 moves 15% to 20% in a year, Bitcoin far more. Détente names the real thing danger: the chance your capital goes to zero. The two are different.

A bond from a badly run state pays the same coupon every year, then one Monday it pays zero. A salary arrives every month, then one Monday it stops. A taxi driver's income jumps around all year, and Paris keeps taking taxis.

Low movement can hide high danger. High movement can carry low danger.

Détente · Gave
10

Accept movement, refuse danger.

Higher expected return comes with higher movement. Détente's test: Bernard Madoff showed 10% a year on a flat line. A flat line paying 10% breaks the physics of markets, and it was fraud.

So accept the movement. Limit the danger with two tools: strong collateral (a building keeps a value), and liquidity (a digital asset trades 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, so you can leave).

Détente
11

Prudence is the trap.

Prudence means repeating what worked over the last 40 years. In a changing world, that is a bet that the world stays still.

Détente's image: the grandfather is an eagle, the son a falcon, the grandson a pigeon, and pigeons get plucked. He cites the parable of the talents: the servant who buried his coin to stay safe was the one punished.

80% of the civilizations that existed have vanished. Taking the risk gives about 20% odds of surviving a change. Refusing the risk gives 0%.

Détente
04

Four tools to build the portfolio

Détente's compass
12

Value moves. Bet on where it goes.

First compass point. The car maker ruined the cart maker. Value moves toward new things, and now it moves faster: technology adoption follows S-curves, and the curves now overlap.

Two of Détente's examples. AI unlocks robotics and biology: AlphaFold folded 200 million proteins in 15 days. Compute is becoming an asset class, and Larry Fink of BlackRock said so; a new asset class appears about every 30 years.

The bet on where value goes is yours to take. It is the one bet that is yours alone.

Détente
13

In a storm, hold antifragile assets.

Second compass point, from Nassim Taleb, measured by Didier Darcet. A fragile asset gains when markets are calm: a stock index. An antifragile asset gains when markets shake: gold, the yen, Bitcoin, homes in Switzerland or Dubai.

Each time Europe shakes, wealthy Europeans move and Swiss prices rise. The test is a slope: plot return against volatility, and the two kinds of asset point in opposite directions.

If you answered "the world is changing" in step 00, you want antifragile.

Détente
14

Decorrelation is the one free lunch.

Third compass point. Milton Friedman said finance has one free lunch: assets that move for different reasons. Add waves that peak at different times and the sum is flatter. Your maximum loss shrinks and your return stays.

Bitcoin is the least correlated asset over long series. Hold only S&P 500 stocks and, the day the index cracks, all of it cracks.

Your brain fights this. A flat in Singapore, a datacenter in Texas and a Bitcoin company in one portfolio offend everyone. That is the sign it works.

Détente
15

Size by proportion, then place the pieces where blocs meet.

Fourth compass point. A bet is a size, and the size replaces the yes or no. Détente's example: gold's market value against Bitcoin's gives about 2%. Put 2% in. Lose it and you live. Win and the portfolio doubles.

Three assets at 40%, 30% and 20% a year: if one goes to zero, the other two repay it in a year.

Then place the pieces. The world splits into blocs, the United States and China first. Gave and Détente both point to hinge places where the blocs still talk: Switzerland, Singapore, Dubai, Miami, Hong Kong. Money there is left alone, and it is where wealth goes when the rest shakes.

Détente · Gave
05

One portfolio, as an example

The three burrows

Détente's own three pockets. Each sits where value moves, gains in a storm, and moves for its own reasons.

Pocket 1 · Compute
Energy plus datacenters
Bought at book value, while CoreWeave trades at 11 times book.
Pocket 2 · Hinge cities
Luxury homes, off plan
For people who live in 2 to 4 countries a year.
Pocket 3 · Amplified Bitcoin
Treasury companies, plus options
About 10% of the pocket in call options for convexity.

Under it, Gave's base layer: stocks, gold, cash and bonds picked by the square, 25% in energy, dividends when inflation rises.

Under everything, Dalio's two rules: earn more than you spend, and treat each other well.

06

Rules of the road

  • Strong convictions, and change your mind when conditions change. Détente
  • The blow comes from where you looked least. List the risks; the one that kills you is off the list. Détente
  • Transitions are the hardest moments. Gave
  • 3% of assets moving is enough to move value. Change comes from the edge. Détente
  • When gold leaves a country, leave with it. Gold knows first. Gave
