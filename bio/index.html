04

How to read it

  • The morning afterA day is judged by the night that follows it: a late coffee on Tuesday is scored against Wednesday's sleep. The night itself (bedtime, wake-up, hours slept) is scored against the morning it ends.
  • More against less, later against earlierAn on/off thing splits on itself. A count or a clock time splits at my own median, so "a later last coffee" means later than my usual.
  • Five stepsVery bad, bad, neutral, good, very good. Good means good for that result, not a bigger number: a resting heart rate going down is good. "Very" means a large gap (Cohen's d of 0.8 or more). For weight, lower is good; a heavier morning after more water is water, not fat.
  • The strength labelHow far apart the two groups sit, measured against how much that number swings day to day anyway. A big percent on a wild metric says less than a small one on a steady metric.
  • When a cell is neutralOver a hundred links are tested, so some look real by luck. A cell leaves neutral only when it clears a false-discovery check, which keeps luck to at most 1 in 10 of what is coloured. A fraction like 2/6 is a cell still gathering comparable days; a dot, one with enough days but fewer than 3 on one side. The grid opens on day 2 and fills in as the days add up; a faint dashed colour shows which way a neutral cell is leaning, before it is clear of luck.
  • Linked things move togetherA late third meal is also more calories, so "a later last meal" and "more calories eaten" can both claim the same kilo. When two rows tell one story, change one of them alone for a week.
  • One month only1 to 31 October 2026. Thirty-one days, one video a day, then the table stands as it is.
